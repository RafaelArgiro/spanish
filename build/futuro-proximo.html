0What the Futuro Próximo Is Used ForEl futuro próximo — usos

The futuro próximo is the Spanish equivalent of English “going to”. It is the ordinary, everyday way of talking about the future in speech, and in most of Latin America it has largely displaced the one-word future tense in conversation.

It is built from three pieces and only the first of them changes. That makes it the fastest route to talking about the future: if you can conjugate ir, you can put any verb in the language into the future without learning a single new ending.

The five main uses

Vamos a — two readings

Vamos a comer can mean “we are going to eat” or “let's eat”. Tone and context separate them, and in practice the ambiguity almost never causes trouble.

1The FormulaIr + a + infinitivo

ir  +  a  +  infinitivo
only the first word changes

Three pieces, in a fixed order. The verb ir is conjugated in the present tense to match the subject. The a is a fixed preposition that never changes. The main verb is left in the infinitive — exactly as it appears in the dictionary.

Step 1 — conjugate ir in the present

This is the only irregular part of the whole construction, and it is one of the twelve verbs you already memorised for the present tense.

yotúél / ella / ustednosotrosvosotrosellos / ellas / ustedes
voyvasva vamosvaisvan

The present forms of ir come from a different Latin verb than the infinitive, which is why they share no letters with ir. There is nothing to work out; they have to be known by heart.

Step 2 — add a

The a sits between the conjugated ir and the infinitive. It carries no meaning of its own and it never changes form.

The single most common mistake

Leaving out the a. English says “I am going to speak” with no visible preposition joining the two verbs, and learners copy that structure directly.

Voy hablar con él is wrong. It must be Voy a hablar con él.

Step 3 — add the infinitive

The main verb stays in its dictionary form: ending in -ar, -er or -ir. It is not conjugated, it does not agree with anything, and it is identical for every person.

The full form

Person -ARhablar · to speak -ERcomer · to eat -IRabrir · to open
yovoy a hablarvoy a comervoy a abrir
túvas a hablarvas a comervas a abrir
él / ella / ustedva a hablarva a comerva a abrir
nosotrosvamos a hablarvamos a comervamos a abrir
vosotrosvais a hablarvais a comervais a abrir
ellos / ellas / ustedesvan a hablarvan a comervan a abrir

Read down any column: the infinitive in teal is identical in all six rows. Read across any row: the form of ir in red is identical for all three verb groups. The -ar / -er / -ir distinction, which mattered so much in the present tense, does no work at all here.

Voy a hablar con el director mañana.I am going to speak to the director tomorrow. ¿Vais a comer con nosotros?Are you going to eat with us? Van a abrir una tienda nueva.They are going to open a new shop.

Why the infinitive makes this easy

Every difficulty from the present tense disappears. Stem changes, irregular yo forms and spelling changes all live in the conjugated verb — and the conjugated verb here is always ir. The main verb is never touched.

The practical consequence

A verb you have only ever seen in the dictionary can be used correctly in the futuro próximo straight away. You do not need to know whether it is regular, stem-changing or irregular, because none of that information is ever used.

Ir in the futuro próximo

Ir can be its own infinitive: Voy a ir al cine is correct and common. What you cannot do is drop one of them — Voy al cine means “I am going to the cinema” in the present, which is usually close enough that speakers simply use it.

Voy a ir al cine el sábado.I am going to go to the cinema on Saturday. ¿Vas a ir a la fiesta?Are you going to go to the party?

2Negation, Questions and PronounsNegación, preguntas y pronombres

Negation

No goes in front of the conjugated ir, never between the pieces of the construction.

No voy a trabajar el domingo.I am not going to work on Sunday. Ella no va a venir.She is not going to come.

Questions

Nothing changes in the verb. A question is marked by intonation and punctuation, with the question word — if there is one — at the front.

¿Vas a venir?Are you going to come? ¿Cuándo vas a llamarme?When are you going to call me? ¿Qué van a hacer este fin de semana?What are they going to do this weekend?

Pronouns have two possible positions

Object pronouns and reflexive pronouns can either go before the conjugated ir or be attached to the end of the infinitive. Both are fully correct and both are common. What you may not do is place the pronoun between a and the infinitive.

StructureRuleBoth of these are correct
Direct objectBefore voy, or attached to the infinitiveLo voy a comprar. / Voy a comprarlo.
Indirect objectSame two optionsTe voy a escribir. / Voy a escribirte.
ReflexiveSame two optionsMe voy a duchar. / Voy a ducharme.
Two pronounsThey move together and keep their orderTe lo voy a dar. / Voy a dártelo.
NegativeNo goes first, the pronoun stays with the verb it choseNo me voy a duchar. / No voy a ducharme.
Never between a and the infinitive

Voy a me duchar and Voy a lo comprar are both wrong. The pronoun must either move all the way to the front or attach to the back of the infinitive.

Reflexive verbs

A reflexive verb behaves exactly like any other. The pronoun still has to agree with the subject, whichever position you choose for it.

Person Pronoun first Attached to the infinitive

túte vas a levantarvas a levantarte

nosotrosnos vamos a levantarvamos a levantarnos
vosotrosos vais a levantarvais a levantaros
ellos / ellas / ustedesse van a levantarvan a levantarse
When an accent is needed

Attaching one pronoun to an infinitive never needs an accent: comprarlo, ducharme, escribirte, levantarse.

Attaching two pronouns does, because the stress would otherwise shift: dártelo, decírmelo, comprárselo.

Me voy a acostar temprano. / Voy a acostarme temprano.I am going to go to bed early. No te lo voy a decir. / No voy a decírtelo.I am not going to tell you.

3Futuro Próximo vs. Futuro SimpleDos maneras de hablar del futuro

Spanish has a second future, the futuro simple, formed as a single word by adding endings to the whole infinitive: hablaré, comerás, abrirá. The two overlap heavily, and in most sentences about future events either one is acceptable.

If you are unsure, use the futuro próximo

In speech it is by a wide margin the more common of the two, and in Latin America the one-word future is rare in conversation outside fixed expressions. It is also the safer choice while you are learning, because its only irregularity is a verb you already know.

What only the futuro simple can do

The one-word future has a second job that the futuro próximo cannot take over: expressing a supposition about the present. English does this with “must be” or “probably”.

¿Qué hora es? — Serán las tres.What time is it? — It must be about three. ¿Dónde está Marta? — Estará en casa.Where is Marta? — She is probably at home.

Van a ser las tres would mean “it is about to be three o'clock” — a statement about the future, not a guess about now.

4Iba a + InfinitivoEl futuro próximo en el pasado

Put ir into the imperfect instead of the present and the whole construction shifts into the past. It then describes something that was going to happen — typically a plan that was interrupted, abandoned or overtaken by events.

The imperfect of ir

Unlike the present, the imperfect of ir is almost regular; only the written accent on the nosotros form needs attention.

yotúél / ella / ustednosotrosvosotrosellos / ellas / ustedes
ibaibasiba íbamosibaisiban

How it is used

The structure is identical — iba + a + infinitive — and the pronoun rules from section 2 carry over unchanged.

Iba a llamarte pero me quedé sin batería.I was going to call you but my battery died. Íbamos a salir pero empezó a llover.We were going to go out but it started raining. ¿Qué ibas a decir?What were you going to say? Se iba a mudar a Madrid pero cambió de idea.She was going to move to Madrid but changed her mind.

A useful pattern

Iba a … pero … is one of the most productive sentence frames in spoken Spanish. It lets you explain an unfulfilled intention without needing any tense you have not already met.

Three tenses of ir, three meanings
  • Voy a estudiar. — I am going to study. plan now
  • Iba a estudiar. — I was going to study. plan then, usually unfulfilled
  • Fui a estudiar. — I went to study. this is literal movement, not a future

The preterite fui a is not a tense of this construction at all — it is the ordinary verb ir meaning physical movement towards an activity.

5Common MistakesErrores frecuentes

Five things go wrong in practice. Every one of them is mechanical, and none involves the main verb.

Not thisBut thisWhy
Voy hablar con él.Voy a hablar con él.The a is obligatory and has no English equivalent to remind you of it.
Voy a hablo con él.Voy a hablar con él.The second verb stays in the infinitive. Only ir is conjugated.
Nosotros va a salir.Nosotros vamos a salir.Ir still has to agree with the subject.
Voy a me duchar.Me voy a duchar. / Voy a ducharme.A pronoun never sits between a and the infinitive.
Voy a voy al cine.Voy a ir al cine.The infinitive of ir is ir, not a second conjugated form.
The one to watch

Dropping the a accounts for more errors than the other four combined, and it survives a long time because the sentence still sounds almost right. Build the habit of hearing voy a, vas a, va a as single units rather than as two separate words.

Va a and vamos a run together in speech

Native speakers blend the vowels: va a hablar comes out close to va’blar. This is why the a is so easy to miss when listening, and why it is worth writing out deliberately until it is automatic.

What to carry away

  • The futuro próximo has no new endings to learn. Everything rests on six forms of ir you already know.
  • Because the main verb stays in the infinitive, no stem change or irregular yo form ever applies. Any verb in the dictionary can be used immediately.
  • The a is obligatory. It is the only part of the construction that carries no meaning and the only part learners leave out.
  • Pronouns have two equally correct positions, and never the one in the middle.
  • Swapping voy for iba turns the whole thing into “was going to” at no extra cost.
The one thing to take away

Learn voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van and you can put every verb in Spanish into the future. No other tense gives you that much reach for that little effort.
